## Why the Company won

### Three main developments of the 18th century

1. Decline of the Mughals
2. Rise of autonomous states
3. Rise of British supremacy

### 1. Decline of the Mughals

The decline started after Aurangzeb. Reasons:

- Empire too vast to hold together — an "inflated balloon"
- Weak successors
- Military expenditure on unnecessary wars — the "Deccan ulcer"
- Court politics — nobles split into four factions: **Turanis, Iranis, Afghans and Hindustanis**. Princes became pawns in the hands of the nobles, who ruled the kingdom to suit their own agenda.

### 2. Rise of autonomous states

Mughal decline led to the rise of autonomous states, in three categories:

| Category | States |
|---|---|
| Successor kingdoms | Awadh, Hyderabad, Bengal (founder: Murshid Quli Khan) |
| New kingdoms | Marathas, Jats, Sikhs, Afghans |
| Independent kingdoms | Mysore, Kerala, Rajputs |

### 3. Rise of British supremacy

The British saw an opportunity in this situation; their conquest started from the south and from Bengal.

### Later Mughals (1707–1857)

Twelve successors, starting with Bahadur Shah I.

**Why the early successors pacified the Marathas, Rajputs and Jats:** they understood the repercussions of Aurangzeb's hostile policy. Winning these groups over was necessary to retain the territories — otherwise they would assert independence.

#### Bahadur Shah I

- Eldest surviving son of Aurangzeb
- Pacifying policy towards opponents:
  - released Shahu — Shahu, along with the Peshwas, later built a strong Maratha confederacy
  - Shahu won the succession fight against Tarabai with the help of **Balaji Vishwanath**, so he made Balaji his Peshwa
  - friendly treaties with the Jats, Bundelas and Rajputs
- Could not keep peace with the Sikhs → war with Banda Bahadur
- Called *Shah-i-Bekhabar* ("heedless king")
- Ruled for 5 years

#### Jahandar Shah

- Less able son of Bahadur Shah I, but backed by a powerful noble, **Zulfiqar Khan**, who was made his wazir and effectively ran the state
- Continued the pacifying policy:
  - **Marathas:** confirmed the *chauth* and *sardeshmukhi* of the Deccan granted to Shahu by Daud Khan (in the Deccan)
  - **Rajputs:** Jai Singh of Amber made governor of Malwa; Raja Ajit Singh of Marwar made governor of Gujarat
  - **Jats and Bundelas:** continued conciliation with Churaman (Jat) and Chhatrasal Bundela
- **Sikhs:** continued the feud
- Abolished *jizya*
- Replaced Todar Mal's land revenue system with *ijara* (revenue farming)
- Was killed; succeeded by Farrukhsiyar

#### Farrukhsiyar

- Put on the throne by the **Sayyid brothers** — one made **wazir**, the other **mir bakshi**
- The Sayyid brothers followed a policy of religious tolerance towards Hindus
- Continued to pacify the Marathas, Rajputs and Jats, but kept fighting the Sikhs
- Under Farrukhsiyar the Mughals won the war against **Banda Bahadur**, who was captured (1715) and executed in Delhi (1716)
- **Royal farman to the EIC:** Farrukhsiyar was suffering from a disease and was cured by a doctor from the East India Company. In return he granted the EIC a royal farman with many trade privileges, including exemption from duties on trade in Bengal. This was the starting point of the feud between the Bengal nawabs and the EIC.
- Married the daughter of Raja Ajit Singh of Marwar
- Wanted to get rid of the Sayyid brothers, but they conspired against him with the help of **Balaji Vishwanath** (Peshwa) and killed him — the **first Mughal emperor to be killed by his nobles**
- The Sayyids then put two incapable rulers on the throne in quick succession — **Rafi-ud-Darajat** and **Rafi-ud-Daula** — both died soon, of consumption and dysentery

#### Muhammad Shah

- Ruled for about 30 years
- Nicknamed **"Rangeela"** (pleasure-loving)
- At first a puppet of the Sayyid brothers; the other nobles disliked this, and he himself wanted to be free of them. With the help of **Nizam-ul-Mulk** and **Muhammad Amin Khan** (Turani nobles) he defeated them.
- Not farsighted — the Mughal empire's decline really set in under him
- Many things happened for the first time in his reign:
  - **Semi-autonomous states carved out:** Nizam-ul-Mulk (Hyderabad), Murshid Quli Khan (Bengal), Saadat Khan (Awadh)
  - **Marathas crossed into the north** for the first time — under **Baji Rao I** they raided up to the gates of Delhi (1737)
  - **Nadir Shah** (1739) captured the north-west, which again became exposed to danger from the north-west; he carried off the **Peacock Throne** and the **Kohinoor**
  - **Ahmad Shah Abdali** first invaded in his period (1748), but was beaten back at Manupur; his big lootings came under the later emperors

#### Ahmad Shah

- **Ahmad Shah Abdali** plundered India twice during his reign
- **Imad-ul-Mulk** was the prominent noble
- Before Imad-ul-Mulk, **Safdarjung** (Nawab of Awadh) was the wazir; the two fought each other
- Ahmad Shah surrendered Multan and Punjab to Abdali
- Imad-ul-Mulk, with the help of the Marathas, dethroned Ahmad Shah and put **Alamgir II** on the throne

#### Alamgir II

- The **Battle of Plassey** (1757) happened during his reign
- **Marathas** under **Raghunath Rao**, guided by Imad-ul-Mulk, captured Delhi and banished the **Rohilla chief** (Najib-ud-Daula)
- They removed Abdali's son from the governorship of Punjab and Multan and put their own man in as governor — this **angered Abdali**
- Later, Imad-ul-Mulk had him **murdered (1759)** and made **Shah Jahan III** emperor — he was weak and soon dethroned

#### Shah Alam II

- Reigned for about **47 years (1759–1806)**, though his real power was brief; two important battles happened in his time:
  - **Third Battle of Panipat (1761):** Marathas defeated; Abdali put Shah Alam on the throne as ruler
  - **Battle of Buxar (1764):** fought along with **Mir Qasim** and the **Nawab of Awadh** — lost. Had to sign the **Treaty of Allahabad**, giving the **diwani rights of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa** to the EIC; in return he was granted Allahabad and lived in its fort.
    - Signed by **Robert Clive** (1765). There were **two** Treaties of Allahabad: one with Shah Alam II (diwani), one with **Shuja-ud-Daula** of Awadh (got Awadh back except Kora and Allahabad; paid war damages).
- **Scindia** later brought him back to Delhi; he was again defeated by the EIC and became a **pensioner** of the Company
- He was blinded by **Ghulam Qadir**, the Rohilla chief (1788)
- The empire was at its weakest — mocked as ruling only **"from Delhi to Palam"**

#### Akbar II

- Short reign
- Gave the title **"Raja"** to **Ram Mohan Roy** and sent him to England to plead for a higher pension
- The British stopped calling themselves subjects of the Mughals

#### Bahadur Shah II

- **Last Mughal emperor**
- Proclaimed **Emperor of India** by the rebels during the **Revolt of 1857**
- Defeated and **deported to Rangoon**
- **Queen Victoria** proclaimed the throne of India
