## Why the Company won

### Three main developments of the 18th century

1. Decline of the Mughals
2. Rise of autonomous states
3. Rise of British supremacy

### 1. Decline of the Mughals

The decline started after Aurangzeb. Reasons:

- Empire too vast to hold together — an "inflated balloon"
- Weak successors
- Military expenditure on unnecessary wars — the "Deccan ulcer"

### 2. Rise of autonomous states

Mughal decline led to the rise of autonomous states, in three categories:

| Category | States |
|---|---|
| Successor kingdoms | Awadh, Hyderabad, Bengal (founder: Murshid Quli Khan) |
| New kingdoms | Marathas, Jats, Sikhs, Afghans |
| Independent kingdoms | Mysore, Kerala, Rajputs |

### 3. Rise of British supremacy

The British saw an opportunity in this situation; their conquest started from the south and from Bengal.

### Later Mughals (1707–1857)

Twelve successors, starting with Bahadur Shah I.

#### Bahadur Shah I

- Eldest surviving son of Aurangzeb
- Pacifying policy towards opponents:
  - released Shahu — Shahu, along with the Peshwas, later built a strong Maratha confederacy
  - friendly treaties with the Jats, Bundelas and Rajputs
- Could not keep peace with the Sikhs → war with Banda Bahadur
- Called *Shah-i-Bekhabar* ("heedless king")
- Ruled for 5 years

#### Jahandar Shah

- Less able son of Bahadur Shah I, but backed by a powerful noble, **Zulfiqar Khan**, who was made his wazir and effectively ran the state
- Continued the pacifying policy:
  - **Marathas:** confirmed the *chauth* and *sardeshmukhi* of the Deccan granted to Shahu by Daud Khan (in the Deccan)
  - **Rajputs:** Jai Singh of Amber made governor of Malwa; Raja Ajit Singh of Marwar made governor of Gujarat
  - **Jats and Bundelas:** continued conciliation with Churaman (Jat) and Chhatrasal Bundela
- **Sikhs:** continued the feud
- Abolished *jizya*
- Replaced Todar Mal's land revenue system with *ijara* (revenue farming)
- Was killed; succeeded by Farrukhsiyar
